Security Explained

Where Does the Security
Come From?

Not from one magic product — from a stack of proven, audited standards: the same encryption banks use, the same locks governments require, and a design where even we can't read your vault.

The 6 Layers That Make It "Bank-Level"

Banks don't trust a single lock. They stack independent layers so that no single failure exposes anything. This is that stack, in plain language.

1. AES-256 encryption — the same cipher banks and the U.S. government use

Every file, photo, message, and note in your vault is scrambled with AES-256 before it is stored. It would take longer than the age of the universe to guess the key by brute force. This is the exact cipher banks use for your money and the NSA approves for top-secret data.

2. TLS 1.3 in transit — an armored truck for your data

Encrypted storage means nothing if data travels in the open. Everything moving between your device and the vault travels over TLS 1.3 — the same protocol that protects online banking. It can't be read or altered on the way.

3. Zero-knowledge design — we can't read your vault, even if asked

Your master password never leaves your device and is never stored on our servers. The encryption key is derived from it on your device (using Argon2id key derivation). That means a breach of our servers, a rogue employee, or even a court order can't unlock your files — only you hold the key.

4. Multi-factor login + per-branch access — "Link A Branch" is a security gate

Sign-in is protected by a second factor (authenticator app, SMS code, or biometrics) plus automatic lockout after repeated failures. And the family tree's "Link A Branch" feature is more than convenience: each branch is its own permission zone. Only people you link to a branch can see that branch — nobody else in the family, and nobody outside it.

5. Tamper-proof audit trail — every access is stamped

Every view, edit, and share is written to an append-only audit log with a timestamp that can't be edited or erased. You can see exactly who opened what and when. This is the same "stamped and timed" principle behind the vault's documents: nothing is ever deleted — earlier versions are locked, sealed, and kept as proof.

6. Certified infrastructure + independent testing

The vault runs on infrastructure certified to SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 — the same certifications auditors demand of banks. Data is encrypted in redundant, geographically separated backups. And security is verified by independent penetration tests, not just our own word.

The Direct Answer: Where Does the Security Come From?

Security is not bought as a single product — it's built from open, peer-reviewed standards:

  • The ciphers come from published, mathematically proven standards (AES, TLS) — reviewed by cryptographers worldwide, not a vendor's secret recipe.
  • The code uses battle-tested, audited encryption libraries (the same ones behind banking apps and password managers), not home-grown crypto.
  • The hosting comes from cloud providers carrying SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certifications — facilities with biometric entry, 24/7 monitoring, and encrypted redundant storage.
  • The proof comes from independent security audits and penetration tests — outside experts paid to try to break in, with reports the vault's owners can share.

"Bank-level" isn't marketing language — it means the same ciphers, the same certifications, and the same independent testing that regulators require of banks.

Security Questions, Answered

Can The Walker Crown Vault team read my documents?

No. With zero-knowledge encryption, your files are encrypted on your device with a key only you hold. We store scrambled data we cannot read — by design, not by policy.

What if I forget my password?

Because we never store it, we can't reset it for you. On signup you receive an Emergency Recovery Kit with a unique recovery key — keep it somewhere physically safe. This is the price of true privacy, and it's how the most secure vaults in the world work.

Can a family member see branches they aren't linked to?

No. "Link A Branch" creates separate permission zones. A person linked to Tyrelle's branch cannot see Aisha's branch unless explicitly linked there too. Access is always opt-in, never inherited.

Can documents really never be erased?

Every edit creates a new version that is stamped with date and time, while the previous version is locked and sealed as read-only. Nothing is overwritten and nothing is deleted — your family history is preserved exactly as it happened. (You can still export or close your whole account; the "never erase" rule protects the record inside the vault.)
